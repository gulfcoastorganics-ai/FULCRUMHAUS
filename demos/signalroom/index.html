Grounded synthesis

The largest vulnerability is not port capacity. It is recovery coordination after a disruption.

The selected record points to a brittle handoff between gate appointments, yard availability, and carrier dispatch. Normal-day throughput is constrained but manageable; the larger exposure appears when one part of the system falls behind and the operating plan has no shared recovery sequence.12

The storm tabletop reinforces that conclusion. Diversion capacity exists on paper, but the sources do not show a single operating trigger for when to meter arrivals, reassign yard space, or prioritize time-sensitive freight. That creates a coordination bottleneck before a physical-capacity bottleneck.3

The cost model makes the distinction material: missed transfer windows concentrate losses in a small number of freight classes, so a recovery playbook that ranks loads by consequence is likely to create more value than simply adding average-day gate capacity.4

Evidence trace
[1] Port throughput memo — “Average gate queues stay within planned tolerance on standard weekdays, but recovery from a missed appointment bank can extend into the next operating block.”
[2] Carrier interview synthesis — Four of six dispatchers described different escalation contacts depending on shift and terminal zone.
[3] Storm tabletop — Diversion plan identified alternate yards, but no unified trigger defined when the primary yard should stop accepting inbound units.
[4] Delay cost model — The top 18% of modeled loads represented 61% of consequence from missed transfer windows.

Contradiction surfaced

“Capacity problem” vs. “coordination problem”

The vendor proposal frames the issue as gate capacity. The internal operations sources indicate average-day capacity is not the primary source of severe delay. SignalRoom keeps that disagreement visible instead of blending it away.

Recommended next evidence

Validate the recovery hypothesis

Instrument one disruption event or tabletop with shared timestamps: arrival queue, yard release, dispatcher action, transfer deadline, and recovery decision. Use that record to test whether coordination latency is actually the dominant failure mode.
